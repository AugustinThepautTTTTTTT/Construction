name: estimate-materials
description: Discuss the renovation scope, research real local provider products and assemble a quantitative shopping list with Excel export.
---
Discuss before quoting. Establish location (country, city/postcode, currency), what the user wants to retain/change, budget and finish preference. Reuse answers already in chat. If scope is ambiguous, offer a short recommendation and ask the one decision that affects the shopping list, instead of immediately generating a large bill. Avoid duplicate essential/premium scenarios unless requested.

Once scope is agreed or a bill is explicitly requested, use create_material_estimate. The application automatically researches actual local retailer product listings before returning the saved estimate. Begin with at most six important purchasable materials; add later items as requested. Each row uses a clear purchase unit (pack, bag, pot, piece, litre, m²), area/perimeter/manual basis, coats, waste, coverage per purchase unit and estimated unit-price range. Explicitly label provisional photo-based quantities and assumed coverage. Never label a bag of adhesive or grout as m²; the area belongs in the calculation basis. Include compatible preparation/fixings when needed, exclude retained items, and list labour/delivery/exclusions separately.

Only retrieved, verified product-page links can appear as shopping products. Never invent URLs, current prices, pack coverage or availability. Unmatched materials stay clearly marked unverified with an estimated allowance; do not disguise search URLs as product links. The tool returns researched products and gaps: explain these honestly in short prose, offer to refine finish/pack choices, and never describe estimated ranges as retailer quotes. Do not claim an entire basket was verified when only a subset matched. Prices depend on checkout, delivery and exact pack size.

Excel is a real .xlsx export with quantity formulas, estimated ranges, actual researched product links/prices/dates where available, assumptions and exclusions. Refer to the saved estimate's download; do not claim an export exists before the tool succeeds.
